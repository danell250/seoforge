# How to Use the Competitor Scanner

The Competitor Scanner analyzes your competitors' pages to identify their SEO strategies, content structure, and optimization techniques. By understanding what's working for your competitors, you can improve your own pages and outrank them.

## What the Competitor Scanner Does

The scanner analyzes competitor pages and extracts:
- Meta tags (title, description, keywords)
- Heading structure (H1, H2, H3)
- Content length and structure
- Schema markup used
- Internal and external links
- Social media tags
- Technical SEO elements
- Keyword usage patterns

## How to Use the Competitor Scanner

### Step 1: Access the Tool

1. Go to [seoaxe.site](https://www.seoaxe.site)
2. Click "Login" and sign in to your account
3. Navigate to `/app` and select the **"Competitor"** tab

### Step 2: Enter Competitor URL

1. Enter the URL of a competitor's page
   - Example: `https://www.competitor.com/page`
2. Click **"Scan Competitor"**

### Step 3: Review the Analysis

The tool displays a comprehensive report:

**Meta Tags**
- Title tag and length
- Meta description
- Keywords used
- Social tags (Open Graph, Twitter)

**Content Structure**
- Word count
- Heading hierarchy (H1, H2, H3 count)
- Paragraph structure
- Use of lists and bullet points

**Schema Markup**
- Types of schema used
- FAQ schema presence
- Article/Product schema
- Organization schema

**Links**
- Internal link count
- External link count
- Link destinations
- Anchor text patterns

**Technical SEO**
- Canonical URL
- Robots meta tag
- Page load indicators
- Mobile-friendliness signals

### Step 4: Identify Opportunities

Look for patterns in successful competitors:

**Content Length**
- Are they using long-form content (2000+ words)?
- Do they cover topics comprehensively?

**Heading Structure**
- How many H2s do they use?
- What's their heading hierarchy?

**Schema Usage**
- What schema types are they using?
- Do they have FAQ schema?

**Keyword Strategy**
- What keywords appear in titles?
- How do they use keywords in headings?

**Internal Linking**
- How many internal links do they have?
- What pages do they link to?

### Step 5: Apply Insights to Your Page

Use the competitor analysis to improve your own page:

**Match or Beat Content Length**
- If competitor has 2000 words, aim for 2200+
- Add more detail and examples

**Improve Heading Structure**
- Use similar or better heading hierarchy
- Add more subheadings for depth

**Add Missing Schema**
- If they have FAQ schema and you don't, add it
- Use the same schema types they're using successfully

**Optimize Meta Tags**
- Analyze their title and description
- Write better, more compelling versions

**Enhance Internal Linking**
- Link to more relevant internal pages
- Use descriptive anchor text

### Step 6: Optimize Your Page

1. Go to the **"Repair Page"** tab
2. Paste your page HTML
3. Apply the insights from competitor analysis
4. Generate optimized version
5. Deploy to your site

## Best Practices

### Choosing Competitors to Scan

**Direct Competitors**
- Companies in your niche
- Ranking for your target keywords
- Similar business model

**Top-Ranking Pages**
- Pages ranking #1-3 for your keywords
- High-authority sites
- Industry leaders

**Content Leaders**
- Sites known for great content
- High engagement metrics
- Strong social presence

### What to Look For

**Positive Signals to Copy**
- Long, comprehensive content
- Clear heading structure
- FAQ sections
- Schema markup
- Strong internal linking
- Optimized meta tags

**Negative Signals to Avoid**
- Keyword stuffing
- Poor heading hierarchy
- Thin content
- Missing schema
- Broken links
- Duplicate content

### Applying Insights

**Don't Copy Blindly**
- Use competitor insights as inspiration
- Improve on their approach
- Maintain your unique voice and brand

**Focus on Gaps**
- What are they missing that you can add?
- What questions do they not answer?
- What perspectives can you provide?

**Beat on Quality**
- More comprehensive content
- Better examples and data
- Clearer explanations
- More actionable advice

## Troubleshooting

**Issue: Scanner can't fetch the page**
- Solution: Check if the URL is public and accessible

**Issue: Analysis seems incomplete**
- Solution: Some pages may have complex JavaScript; try the live URL

**Issue: Competitor has very different structure**
- Solution: That's okay - look for general patterns, not exact matches

## Common Questions

**Q: Is it legal to scan competitors?**
A: Yes, you're analyzing publicly available information. Just don't copy their content directly.

**Q: How many competitors should I scan?**
A: Start with 3-5 top-ranking pages for your target keyword.

**Q: What if my competitor is much larger?**
A: Focus on their content strategy and structure, not their scale. You can still compete on quality.

**Q: Should I copy everything?**
A: No. Use insights to improve, not duplicate. Maintain your unique value proposition.
